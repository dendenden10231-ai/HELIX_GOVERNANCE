It is forbidden to <strong>compute and to decide</strong>. Every number, every threshold, every verdict is produced by deterministic Python whose source is open to line-by-line inspection.</p> <p>This is not an aspiration written in documentation. It is enforced inside the system prompt sent to the model on every single call. Here it is verbatim, from the running file:</p> <div class="spec"> <div class="spec-head"><b>helix_pipeline/engine/governance_classifier.py</b><span class="r">system prompt · verbatim from §16 of the specification</span></div> <div class="spec-body"><span class="c">1. HALLUCINATIONS: identify signals of 7 types (TYPE-1..TYPE-7).</span> Return <span class="k">ONLY text labels and excerpts — NOT numbers.</span> The mathematics (severity weights, confidence) is computed by the Python engine. <span class="c">2. NGI DIMENSIONS: score 4 dimensions on a 0-3 scale.</span> <span class="k">These are the ONLY numbers you return.</span> Justify in prose, without formulas. <span class="w">IMPORTANT:</span> - Do not include confidence numbers, scores, weights — that is the engine - Do not include governance_decision — that is the engine - severity_signal is your semantic reading only, <span class="k">not a final weight</span></div> <div class="spec-cap"><b>What matters here.</b> The model returns exactly four numbers per pass — four 0–3 ratings on a narrative-gravity scale. Everything else, including the final verdict of <em>pass / escalate to a human / block</em>, it is structurally unable to utter. The prompt is not hand-edited: it is kept in sync with §16 of the specification, and any divergence is treated as a defect.</div> </div> <div class="box t"> <div class="box-l">And the mirror rule</div> <p>A missing provider key does not yield an empty result. It yields <code>CLASSIFIER-NOT-CONFIGURED</code> and an exception. An empty list of violations would mean "checked, nothing found" — a conclusion reached without checking. The system refuses to say it.</p> </div> </section> <section class="rv"> <div class="tag"><span class="n">01·5</span> The live system</div> <h2>This is not a specification. The model selects them. The model does not execute them.</p> <p>Ten absolute rules govern that layer. Four of them show the character of the whole system:</p> <div class="spec"> <div class="spec-head"><b>docs/MATH-KERNEL-COMPACT-v0_1.md</b><span class="r">MATH-KERNEL LAW · 4 of 10</span></div> <div class="spec-body"><span class="k">RULE 1 — LLM SELECTS, BACKEND CALCULATES</span> The LLM chooses an OPERATOR ID and forms the call. The LLM does NOT compute the value in latent space. A beautiful number is not evidence: a separate rule forbids a numeric result, on its own, from ever raising the trust level of a conclusion.</div> </div> </section> <section class="rv"> <div class="tag"><span class="n">05</span> The proof</div> <h2>Three times the system caught its own makers</h2> <p class="lede">The easiest claim to fake in work like this is "we checked, it's correct." So here are three documented cases where the checking found a defect inside the system itself. Each with a date, a file, and a fix.</p> <div class="box c"> <div class="box-l">Case 1 · invented thresholds · found by spec-versus-code review</div> <p>The confidence module carried four clean thresholds: HIGH ≥ 0.90, MEDIUM ≥ 0.61, LOW ≥ 0.31, CRITICAL &lt; 0.31. They looked derived. Checked word for word against the specification: <strong>not one of them exists in it.</strong> The specification divides that scale exactly once — <code>if confidence &lt; 0.30: HITL_REQUIRED</code>. Three surplus thresholds had been invented at some earlier step and were living in the code, shaping verdicts.</p> <p>Removed. The function now returns exactly the binary split the specification itself uses. Restoring them would violate P0-6, the project's founding rule: <em>no number without a traceable formula.</em> This is precisely the class of error the whole architecture exists to catch — and this time it caught itself.</p> </div> <div class="box c"> <div class="box-l">Case 2 · 44 operators silently inventing inputs · found by a live production run</div> <p>Local tests were green. The manifest audit was clean. Fifteen demo scenarios against the live endpoint returned fifteen honest <code>200 OK</code>s. Inside sat the defect: the Bayesian update operator, given only a prior, returned <code>result: 0.8</code> with status <code>EXECUTED</code> — while the missing <code>likelihood = 0.8</code> and <code>false_positive_rate = 0.2</code> were <strong>invented by the backend and never shown in the response</strong>. All 44 operators behaved this way.</p> <p>The registry forbids it in as many words: "when priors are unknown, the system must declare NON&nbsp;LIQUET."
